    Mover candidates
    
    
    CatWISE2020 sources in this field whose catalogued motion looks significant, ranked by a
      machine-learning classifier's probability that they really move (by 50 mas/yr or more). Most
      significant-looking motions of faint sources are artifacts; the classifier separates the two.
      Rings on the image mark the candidates listed.

    Talasuri: help
    
  
    
    
    
      
    What this is
    
      
    Talasuri (Tagalog tala, “star”, + suri, “to examine”) gathers every
        SPHEREx image of the patch of sky you choose, lines them up on one grid and plays them in time
        order like a flipbook. SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope that maps the whole sky in infrared light
        (0.75–5 µm) through 102 colours, about twice a year. Things that move or change brightness
        stand out; the tool also measures the target in every image, giving a spectrum and a light curve.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Quick start
    
      
      
        
    1. Pick a target: type a name or coordinates in the search box, or open Sky Sphere or
          Aladin, click the sky and press Load frames here.
      2. 
        
    2. Wait for the frames. A first visit reads a few hundred images from NASA's archive (about a
          minute); frames appear after ~10 s. Return visits take seconds.
      3. 
        
    3. Press ▶, or set Quick view to Highlight movement.
      4. 
      
    
      
    
        
        
        
        
        
        
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    The three views
    
      
      
        
    • Movement Classifier (top left) lists sources in the field that may be moving: CatWISE2020 motions
          ranked by a classifier trained against Gaia and known brown dwarfs. A probability, not a discovery:
          check the flipbook.
      • 
        
    • Interactive Viewer and Characteristics (top left). Interactive Viewer shows just the flipbook and a sky map side by
          side: pick a position, press play. Characteristics adds Settings, Measurements and the full-size
          Sky Sphere and Aladin views.
      • 
        
    • Flipbook: the images. The line under the image gives frame number, date, MJD, detector and
          wavelength; the scale bar sits in the image's corner. The strip under the image has one tick per frame, coloured by detector; amber caps mark
          frames whose target pixel is flagged. North is up, east is left.
      • 
        
    • Sky Sphere: the whole sky for orientation. Inside puts you at the centre of the
          celestial sphere (north up, east left, as the real sky). Outside shows the sky as a globe, which
          is mirror-reversed: east is to the right. Drag to look around, scroll to zoom, click to pick a position.
          Diamonds are preset targets.
      • 
        
    • Aladin: a zoomable survey map (CDS Aladin Lite) with the loaded field and catalogue overlays.
      • 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Reading the images
    
      
      
        
    • Each frame is a different colour. SPHEREx's filters vary across the detector, so stars brighten
          and fade between frames even when nothing changes. Compare frames from one detector, or use stacks.
      • 
        
    • Movement in a difference image is a dark spot (where it was) beside a bright spot (where it is).
      • 
        
    • Pixels are big (6.15″): close pairs blend, and one pixel of motion takes most fast stars a year.
      • 
        
    • Very bright stars saturate: their cores are blank or wrong and the tool warns you. Positions and
          motion are still useful; brightness is not.
      • 
        
    • Images are never tinted. Greyscale is a neutral ramp; the only colour added is the bad-pixel
          highlight you switch on under Stretch.
      • 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Settings
    
      
      
        
    • Field of view: how much sky to load (1–30′) and on-screen magnification.
      • 
        
    • Time and epochs: date range, the window that groups exposures into epochs, frame time.
      • 
        
    • Detectors and wavelength: limit to detectors (each covers a band) or a wavelength range.
      • 
        
    • Stack and difference: every image, stacks per epoch or detector, one wavelength stack, or false
          colour; optionally subtract the median or a reference image.
      • 
        
    • Stretch: brightness scale, black/white levels, invert, and bad-pixel flags.
      • 
        
    • Proper motion: track a moving target; Look up in Gaia DR3 fills the values in.
          Motion prediction (in Measurements) fits the object's WISE epochs instead and marks where it
          should be in each image, with an uncertainty ellipse.
      • 
        
    • Overlays: crosshair, Gaia and CatWISE sources, sphere grid and planes, Aladin imagery.
      • 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Measurements
    
      
    For every image the tool sums the light in a small circle on the target and subtracts the local
        background and the zodiacal glow (forced aperture photometry, in µJy, no aperture correction).
        The spectrum is coloured by epoch (darker = earlier); the light curve is best read over a
        narrow wavelength range. Hollow points touch bad pixels. A neighbour inside the circle adds its light.

        Names and positions: SIMBAD and Sesame (CDS). Sky maps: Aladin Lite (CDS), the SPHEREx DR1 all-sky map (IRSA),
        2MASS, unWISE/AllWISE and DSS. Catalogues: Gaia DR3 (ESA) and CatWISE2020.
